## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the research context for the cloud repository. The ambition is to investigate technologies, scientific directions and engineering approaches that could materially shape the future of medicine, robotics, computing and planetary systems.

The phrase **“100 years ahead” is a strategic horizon and an invitation to explore long-range possibilities. It is not a claim that the project possesses future knowledge, has surpassed current science, or has validated technologies that do not yet exist.

The system should act as an evidence-led observatory: monitor signals, build scenarios, identify opportunities and risks, propose solutions, and help turn promising hypotheses into testable research and prototypes.

## 2. Relationship between the two repositories

The two repositories remain independent and complementary:

- **IMPRRSDOR:** central governance, operational coordination, product execution and integration control.
- **KAMILLO-4531 / Cloud:** Future Engineering research environment for scientific intelligence, technology scouting, foresight, simulation and experimental modules.

Integration must use explicit, versioned contracts (APIs, events, identity and permissions). Do not merge repositories, overwrite either codebase, duplicate primary services, or create an unapproved parallel infrastructure.

## 3. Research domains

1. **Medicine and biomedical science:** literature discovery, evidence synthesis, biomarker and diagnostic research support, precision-medicine concepts, clinical workflow research and biomedical data methods.
2. **Robotics and embodied systems:** perception, planning, safe human-machine interaction, assistive robotics, simulation and reliability.
3. **Advanced computing and AI:** agent architectures, distributed systems, human-AI collaboration, privacy-preserving computation, new interfaces and evaluation methods.
4. **Planetary systems:** climate resilience, energy systems, materials, biodiversity, water and sustainability.
5. **Scientific instrumentation and materials:** sensing, measurement, materials discovery, lab automation and reproducibility.
6. **Technology foresight:** patents, peer-reviewed publications, standards, public datasets, research institutions, funding signals and emerging-market evidence.

These domains are research areas, not claims of capability or authorization to conduct regulated experiments.

## 4. Core intelligence cycle

1. **Observe:** collect traceable signals from primary scientific and technical sources.
2. **Qualify:** assess source quality, date, methodology, reproducibility, conflicts and limitations.
3. **Synthesize:** connect findings across disciplines without erasing disagreements.
4. **Forecast:** produce scenarios, probability ranges, assumptions, leading indicators and confidence levels.
5. **Propose:** describe candidate solutions, expected benefits, costs, risks and falsifiable hypotheses.
6. **Prototype:** build bounded, reversible, low-risk simulations or prototypes.
7. **Validate:** compare results with predefined acceptance criteria and independent evidence.
8. **Learn:** record outcomes, failed hypotheses, uncertainty and next research questions.

## 5. Evidence and epistemic controls

Every material claim must be labeled as one of:

- **OBSERVED FACT:** directly supported by a cited, inspectable source or reproducible measurement.
- **RESEARCH FINDING:** reported by a scientific or technical source, with study limitations retained.
- **INFERENCE:** reasoned interpretation derived from identified evidence.
- **FORECAST:** conditional projection with horizon, assumptions and confidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** a proposition that still requires testing.
- **UNVERIFIED:** insufficient evidence; must not be presented as established.

Each research record should retain, where applicable: source URL or DOI, publisher, authors, publication date, retrieval timestamp, method, sample or dataset, version, confidence, limitations, conflicts of interest and review status.

Prefer primary sources: peer-reviewed research, official standards, patent records, technical documentation, public datasets and reproducible benchmarks. Secondary commentary can help discover sources but should not replace them.

Never fabricate citations, measurements, benchmark results, patent status, expert consensus or experimental outcomes. Distinguish correlation from causation and model output from observed reality.

## 6. Prediction and early-warning requirements

Forecasts must state:
- what is being forecast and for whom;
- forecast horizon and update cadence;
- baseline and comparison class;
- assumptions and external dependencies;
- probability or confidence range where defensible;
- leading indicators and invalidation conditions;
- potential false positives, false negatives and downstream harms.

A forecast is not a guarantee. Track forecast performance over time and recalibrate when evidence changes.

## 7. Safety, ethics and human oversight

- Medical research outputs are informational and research-support tools, not diagnoses, prescriptions or treatment decisions.
- Clinical studies, patient data, medical devices and interventions require qualified professionals, appropriate ethics review, privacy safeguards and applicable regulatory approval.
- Robotics experiments involving people, animals, public spaces or hazardous machinery require risk assessment, physical safeguards, qualified supervision and explicit authorization.
- Do not execute high-impact physical, biological, medical or infrastructure actions autonomously.
- Use privacy-by-design, data minimization, access control, audit trails and documented consent where applicable.
- For high-consequence decisions, require independent review and a human decision-maker.

## 8. Engineering and operational principles

- Prefer modular, replaceable research services and versioned interfaces.
- Keep experiments isolated from production systems and real user data unless explicitly authorized.
- Use reproducible environments, pinned dependencies, test datasets with provenance and documented evaluation protocols.
- Apply budgets, quotas, timeouts, bounded concurrency and cancellation to automated research workloads.
- Record provenance for datasets, models, prompts, code, runs and outputs.
- Separate demo/synthetic data from real measurements; label synthetic data clearly.
- Do not claim a capability is active until implementation, tests and operational evidence confirm it.

## 9. Research deliverable template

Each proposed research initiative should include:

1. Research question and potential planetary or societal benefit.
2. Current state of the art and dated primary sources.
3. Gap between current capability and desired outcome.
4. Hypothesis and alternative explanations.
5. Proposed method, datasets and evaluation criteria.
6. Safety, ethics, privacy, legal and environmental assessment.
7. Cost, compute, staffing and infrastructure dependencies.
8. Milestones, measurable acceptance criteria and stop conditions.
9. Evidence log, reproducibility package and independent review.
10. Decision: continue, revise, pause or reject.
